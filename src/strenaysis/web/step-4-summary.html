Executive summary

This analysis evaluates whether launching a premium recommendation feature will causally increase subscription revenue. The approach is a pre-registered A/B experiment at the account level, powered to detect a 3% lift in net-new MRR within 14 days of first exposure. Three causal mechanisms were identified (discovery lift, salience, self-selection), alongside a critical failure mode: the feature may cannibalize organic discovery rather than create incremental conversions. A 6% identity-graph gap in anonymous sessions poses the largest data risk, since missingness may be patterned rather than random. The decision rule is pre-committed: ship if lift meets threshold and guardrails hold, otherwise redesign. Two blockers remain before launch: confirming the identity-graph join strategy and validating 8+ weeks of clean baseline data.

02

Drivers

Settled

Three causal mechanisms identified: discovery lift (surfacing gated content users wouldn't find), salience effects, and opt-in self-selection. The primary failure mode is cannibalization of organic discovery, where the feature redirects existing paths rather than creating new ones. A novelty-decay analysis is needed to distinguish sustained lift from a temporary engagement spike.

› 3 questions answered
Q
What is the strongest causal mechanism by which premium recommendations would drive new subscriptions?
A
Confirmed
Personalized recommendations surface high-value content behind the paywall, creating a "moment of need" that converts free users who otherwise wouldn't discover it.
Q
What alternative explanations could produce the same metric movement without the feature actually working?
A
Assumption
Novelty effect from any new feature driving temporary engagement. Also possible that the recommendation UI simply increases time-on-site, and it's the dwell time that converts, not the recommendations themselves.
Q
Under what conditions would the feature be live, appear to work, but actually produce no lasting revenue impact?
A
Assumption
If it cannibalizes organic discovery. Users who would have found premium content on their own now find it via recommendations. Net new conversions are zero, but attribution says otherwise.

04

Model

Settled

50/50 A/B at the account level over four weeks, powered for a 3% minimum detectable effect on the primary MRR metric. Account-level randomization avoids within-user contamination from session-level assignment. Network effects are expected to be minimal given the feature is personalized to individual consumption patterns.

› 3 questions answered
Q
What randomization unit will you use, and why?
A
Confirmed
Account-level. Session-level would contaminate since a user could see recommendations in one session and convert in the next. Cluster randomization adds complexity we don't need here.
Q
What sample size do you need to detect a 3% MRR lift with 80% power?
A
Confirmed
~48,000 accounts per arm based on current conversion rates and variance. We reach that in ~10 days of traffic.
Q
How will you handle network effects or spillover between treatment and control groups?
A
Confirmed
Minimal concern. The recommendation feature is individual-consumption-based, not social. No shared content or viral loops. We'll monitor for cross-group information leakage in shared Slack channels but expect it to be negligible.

05

Decision

Settled

Ship if MRR lift is 3% or above and all guardrail metrics hold. If lift is positive but below threshold, redesign the feature and re-test. If guardrails move in the wrong direction regardless of lift, halt and investigate. The decision rule is pre-registered and signed before launch. VP of Product has final authority for ambiguous outcomes.

› 3 questions answered
Q
Write the exact decision rule: under what conditions do you ship, iterate, or kill the feature?
A
Confirmed
Ship if lift ≥ 3% and guardrails hold. Redesign if lift is 0-3%. Kill if guardrails (churn, support tickets) worsen regardless of lift.
Q
Who has final decision authority, and what happens if the data is ambiguous?

VP of Product. If ambiguous, we extend the experiment by 2 weeks. If still ambiguous, default is to not ship.

07

Takeaway

Settled

The premium recommendation feature deserves a credible causal test rather than a directional dashboard read, because an identity-graph ambiguity can produce the exact shape of lift we'd expect to see, even if the feature is doing nothing. Measure the effect, not the feature.

› 2 questions answered
Q
Write the one-sentence takeaway. It should be specific enough to act on and memorable enough to stick.
A
Confirmed
Measure the effect, not the feature, and name the identity-graph ambiguity before the postmortem does.
Q
What is the most important caveat or limitation someone citing this result should keep in mind?
A
Confirmed
The 6% identity-graph gap means our treatment effect estimate is conditional on matched users only. If the unmatched 6% respond differently, the true population effect could be higher or lower than what we measure.
